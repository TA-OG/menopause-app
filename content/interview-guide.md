# Pamela interview guide — turning expertise into app content

This is the script the **host** reads during each Google Meet with Pamela.
Fathom records and transcribes; the transcript is then converted into the
app's wellness frameworks and Learn articles. Pamela never sees any code —
she just talks. The host's only job is to work down this page.

---

## How each session works

1. **One topic per session** (e.g. "hot flushes", "sleep", "mood & anxiety").
   Keep them separate so each transcript maps cleanly to one framework.
2. **Turn Fathom on** before you start. Say the topic name aloud first so the
   transcript is labelled.
3. For each topic, ask the **two closed questions** (who is it for), then the
   **four open prompts** (what helps). Let Pamela talk as long as she likes
   after each — the elaboration is the gold.
4. Don't rush her into categories. If she rambles across food and mindset in
   one breath, that's fine — the conversion step sorts it.

---

## The per-topic block  (repeat for every symptom/topic)

> **Topic:** ____________________  (say it aloud for Fathom)

### Closed questions — "who is this for?"
These map to what the app already asks users at sign-up. Read them as written.

1. **"Who is this advice mainly for?** Is it for women in perimenopause, in
   menopause, postmenopause, after surgical menopause — or all of them?"

2. **"Does the advice change depending on how they're doing in other areas** —
   their sleep, their stress, how active they are, their diet? If so, how?"

*(Optional follow-ups when relevant:)*
   - "Is there anyone this is **not** safe or suitable for?"
   - "How severe does it need to be before this really matters?"

### Open prompts — "what helps?"
Let her narrate freely. Prompt gently through the four areas if she misses one.

3. **Food & diet** — *"What should she eat, or avoid, for this? Anything
   specific — foods, drinks, timing, amounts?"*

4. **Lifestyle** — *"What about daily habits — movement, sleep routine, work,
   environment, things she can change day to day?"*

5. **Mindset & emotional** — *"What would you want her to understand or believe
   about this? How should she think about it, and when should she see her GP?"*

6. **Supplements** — *"Are there any supplements you'd mention? What dose, what
   to look for — and what's the safety warning you'd always give?"*
   *(Every supplement needs a GP-check caveat — make sure she says one.)*

### Close
   - *"If you could say one thing to a woman struggling with this, what is it?"*
     (This often becomes the headline of the Learn article.)

## "Who is this for?" — the answer buckets the app actually uses

When Pamela describes who advice is for, these are the exact options users pick
from at sign-up. Her answers get mapped onto these, so it helps if the host
nudges her toward this language.

**Menopause stage:** not sure yet · perimenopause · menopause · postmenopause ·
surgical menopause

**Symptoms (their main concern):** hot flushes · night sweats · sleep problems ·
mood changes / irritability · anxiety · brain fog / memory · weight changes ·
joint or muscle aches · low energy / fatigue · low libido · vaginal dryness ·
skin changes · hair changes

**Sleep quality:** good · fair · poor · very poor
**Stress level:** low · moderate · high · very high
**Activity level:** very active · moderately active · lightly active ·
not very active · limited by a condition
**Diet:** mostly whole foods · mixed · convenience/fast food · specific diet
(vegan etc.) · doesn't pay attention

**Heritage / culture:** Africa · Caribbean · South Asia · East & SE Asia ·
Middle East & North Africa · Latin America · Europe & British Isles · mixed/other
*(Cultural nuance — foods, traditions, family context — gets its own sessions.)*

---

## Suggested running order

1. **Foundations** — what every woman should do, regardless of symptoms.
   (Becomes the universal framework shown to everyone.)
2. One session per **symptom** from the list above.
3. **Cultural** sessions — for each heritage, how does the advice shift?
   What foods, traditions, family or community factors matter?
4. **Deep-dive articles** — any topic Pamela wants to explain at length
   (these become Learn articles rather than plan recommendations).

---

## After each session

The host hands the Fathom transcript over for conversion. Pamela then gets a
**plain-English review sheet** (generated by `npm run review-frameworks`) showing
exactly what the app will say — "Shown when… we recommend…" — so she can confirm
accuracy or send corrections as more voice notes. She never reads YAML.
